# Escape Startup Lab

# 1. Game Overview
## Title

**Escape Startup Lab**

---

## Genre

Educational Escape Room / Puzzle / Decision-Making Game

---

## Target Audience

High school students (Grades 10–12) participating in entrepreneurship learning activities, workshops, or business plan competitions.

---

## Platform

Web Browser (Desktop/Laptop)

Developed using Phaser 3 and playable through modern web browsers (Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Mozilla Firefox).

---

## Duration

15–20 minutes

The game is designed as a short challenge suitable for use as one station in a rally game or entrepreneurship event.

---

## Number of Players

3–5 students per team

Players collaborate to solve puzzles, analyze information, and make entrepreneurial decisions within the allotted time.

---

## Core Objective

Players must escape the Startup Lab by completing a series of entrepreneurship-themed challenges.

Each challenge requires players to demonstrate entrepreneurial thinking, including opportunity recognition, customer understanding, creativity, resource management, and strategic decision-making.

The objective is not merely to answer questions correctly, but to think and act like entrepreneurs.

---

## Learning Objectives

After completing the game, participants should be able to:

- Recognize business opportunities from everyday problems.
- Distinguish assumptions from validated customer insights.
- Evaluate alternative business decisions based on evidence.
- Allocate limited resources strategically.
- Understand that successful entrepreneurship requires experimentation, critical thinking, and continuous validation.

---

## Educational Competencies

The game assesses the following entrepreneurial competencies:

1. Opportunity Recognition
2. Customer Discovery
3. Creative Problem Solving
4. Decision Making Under Constraints
5. Business Validation

---

## Unique Selling Points

Unlike traditional entrepreneurship quizzes, Escape Startup Lab immerses students in an interactive escape-room experience where every puzzle represents a real entrepreneurial challenge.

Rather than testing memorized knowledge, the game evaluates entrepreneurial thinking through exploration, collaboration, evidence gathering, and decision making.

The game also produces an objective score that can be used directly by competition committees during rally-style events.

---

## Success Criteria

Players successfully complete the game by escaping all challenge rooms before the timer expires.

Performance is evaluated using an automated scoring system based on:

- Puzzle completion
- Decision quality
- Exploration
- Time efficiency

The final score is displayed immediately for committee recording.

---

## Design Principles

The game is designed according to the following principles:

1. Learn by Doing
Students learn entrepreneurship by making decisions rather than answering theoretical questions.

2. Team Collaboration
Every puzzle encourages discussion and collective decision making.

3. Fair Assessment
All scoring criteria are objective, transparent, and automatically calculated.

4. Engagement First
The game should feel like an escape room rather than an examination.

5. Replayability
Randomized scenarios encourage replay and reduce memorization.

---

## 2. Story
# 2. Story

## Story Overview

In the near future, the world's leading entrepreneurs are no longer selected through interviews, written examinations, or business plan presentations. Instead, they are invited to participate in a confidential assessment conducted by **Startup Lab**, an innovation center designed to evaluate entrepreneurial thinking through real-world simulations.

Upon entering the facility, each team is welcomed as a **Candidate Team**. Moments later, the entrance door locks automatically and a holographic artificial intelligence (AI) appears.

The AI introduces itself as the **Entrepreneurial Virtual Assistant (EVA)** and explains that Startup Lab does not measure how much participants know about entrepreneurship—it measures **how they think**.

To complete the assessment, candidates must solve a series of entrepreneurship-themed challenges. Every puzzle represents a real entrepreneurial situation requiring observation, analysis, teamwork, and strategic decision-making.

Only candidates who successfully complete every challenge will receive Founder Certification and unlock the exit.

---

## Narrative Goals

The story is designed to:

- Motivate students through an immersive assessment scenario rather than a traditional quiz.
- Create a sense of urgency without introducing fear or violence.
- Encourage teamwork and discussion during decision-making.
- Reinforce the idea that entrepreneurship is a way of thinking rather than a collection of memorized theories.

---

## Main Character

The player represents a **Candidate Team** participating in the Startup Lab Founder Assessment.

Rather than controlling a specific individual, the game assumes that all team members collaborate to investigate clues and make entrepreneurial decisions together.

---

## Supporting Character

### EVA (Entrepreneurial Virtual Assistant)

EVA is the AI facilitator responsible for guiding candidates throughout the assessment.

Its responsibilities include:

- Explaining the rules.
- Providing hints when necessary.
- Giving feedback after important decisions.
- Unlocking new challenge rooms.
- Delivering the final evaluation.

EVA never directly tells players whether they are "right" or "wrong." Instead, it provides constructive feedback that encourages entrepreneurial thinking.

Example:

> "Interesting choice. Building before validating customer needs is common among early-stage startups. Let's see how your decision affects the outcome."

---

## Story Progression

The game follows a simple three-act structure.

### Opening

Candidates arrive at Startup Lab to participate in the Founder Assessment.

EVA welcomes the team and explains the assessment process before the entrance door locks automatically.

The first challenge room is unlocked.

---

### Challenge Phase

Candidates complete three entrepreneurship challenge rooms:

1. **The Idea Lab**
   - Identify opportunities from customer problems.

2. **The Customer Hub**
   - Validate assumptions using customer evidence.

3. **The Founder Boardroom**
   - Make strategic business decisions under limited resources.

Each completed room demonstrates another aspect of entrepreneurial competency.

---

### Ending

After completing all challenge rooms, EVA performs a final evaluation.

The Candidate Team receives:

- Final Score
- Founder Profile
- Performance Summary

If all required challenges have been completed, the exit door unlocks and EVA congratulates the team.

The assessment is revealed to have been the admission process for Startup Lab rather than an escape challenge.

---

## Story Tone

The narrative should feel:

- Professional
- Encouraging
- Futuristic
- Lighthearted
- Motivating

The game should never feel frightening or stressful like a horror escape room.

Instead, it should resemble a prestigious innovation center evaluating future entrepreneurs.

---

1. Startup Lab logo animation.
2. EVA welcomes the Candidate Team.
3. Brief explanation of the Founder Assessment.
4. Simple gameplay instructions.

The introduction should be concise to maximize gameplay time within the 20-minute session.

# 3. Gameplay Loop

## Gameplay Overview

Escape Startup Lab follows a simple and consistent gameplay loop designed for short educational sessions.

Players investigate each challenge room, gather information, discuss possible solutions as a team, and make entrepreneurial decisions. Every completed room unlocks the next stage of the Founder Assessment.

The gameplay emphasizes observation, critical thinking, collaboration, and decision-making rather than fast reflexes or gaming experience.

---

## Core Gameplay Loop

```text
Main Menu
    ↓
Story Introduction
    ↓
Explore the Room
    ↓
Investigate Objects
    ↓
Collect Clues
    ↓
Discuss with Team
    ↓
Make a Decision
    ↓
Receive Feedback
    ↓
Room Score
    ↓
Next Room
    ↓
Final Evaluation
```

Each challenge room follows the same gameplay structure to minimize the learning curve and maximize time spent solving entrepreneurship problems.

---

## Room Gameplay Flow

Each room consists of five stages.

### Stage 1 – Exploration

Players enter a themed room containing multiple interactive objects.

Examples include:

- Whiteboard
- Laptop
- Customer Survey
- Notebook
- Phone
- Calendar
- Sticky Notes
- Prototype

Players may inspect the objects in any order.

---

### Stage 2 – Investigation

Clicking an object reveals information.

Information may include:

- Customer interview results
- Sales reports
- Business news
- Product sketches
- Financial data
- Market observations

Some objects contain useful clues, while others provide supporting context.

Players are encouraged to investigate thoroughly before making a decision.

---

### Stage 3 – Team Discussion

After gathering sufficient information, players discuss the available evidence.

Because the game is designed for teams, discussion is encouraged before selecting an answer.

No time is paused during discussion.

---

### Stage 4 – Decision Making

Players answer one or more entrepreneurship challenges based on the information collected.

Challenges may include:

- Identifying customer problems
- Choosing the best business strategy
- Prioritizing limited resources
- Evaluating market evidence

Different answers receive different scores based on entrepreneurial reasoning.

---

### Stage 5 – Feedback & Evaluation

After submitting a decision, EVA provides immediate feedback.

Feedback explains the entrepreneurial reasoning behind the selected choice rather than simply indicating whether it is correct.

Players then receive their room score before progressing to the next challenge.

---

## Player Interaction

The game uses a point-and-click interface.

Players interact using a mouse only.

Available interactions include:

- Click object
- Read information
- Close information window
- Select an answer
- Continue dialogue

No keyboard movement or character control is required.

---

## Team Collaboration

Escape Startup Lab is designed primarily for team-based play.

Although only one player controls the mouse, every team member is expected to contribute by:

- Observing clues
- Discussing evidence
- Evaluating alternatives
- Reaching a collective decision

The game rewards thoughtful discussion over rapid guessing.

---

## Challenge Progression

The Founder Assessment consists of three challenge rooms.

1. **The Idea Lab**
   - Identify entrepreneurial opportunities.

2. **The Customer Hub**
   - Validate customer needs using available evidence.

3. **The Founder Boardroom**
   - Make strategic business decisions under limited resources.

Each room introduces increasingly complex entrepreneurial scenarios while maintaining the same interaction pattern.

---

## Win Condition

Players successfully complete the Founder Assessment by:

- Completing all three challenge rooms.
- Achieving a final score.
- Reaching the final evaluation before the timer expires.

---

## Lose Condition

The assessment ends when:

- The global timer reaches zero before all rooms are completed.

Even if the timer expires, players still receive a final evaluation and performance summary.

---

## Design Principles

The gameplay loop is designed according to the following principles:

- Easy to learn within one minute.
- Consistent across all challenge rooms.
- Encourage observation before decision-making.
- Encourage teamwork and discussion.
- Assess entrepreneurial thinking instead of memorized knowledge.
- Minimize unnecessary game mechanics to maximize educational value.

---

## Additional Feature: Candidate Notebook

Instead of allowing students to answer immediately after reading a clue, they first collect important findings into a digital notebook.

✓ Customers complain about waiting time.
✓ Most customers are students.
✓ Peak hours: 11:30–13:00.
□ Sales data (not reviewed yet).

Then, when they click "Answer Challenge", they can quickly review the notebook instead of reopening every object.

# 4. Core Mechanics

## Overview

Escape Startup Lab is designed around a small set of core gameplay mechanics that are easy to learn yet encourage observation, collaboration, and entrepreneurial thinking.

Rather than focusing on complex controls or action-based gameplay, players interact with the environment, gather evidence, and make strategic decisions based on the information they discover.

---

## 4.1 Point-and-Click Interaction

The game uses a simple point-and-click interface.

Players interact with the environment by clicking on objects within each challenge room.

Interactive objects are visually highlighted when hovered over with the cursor.

Examples of interactive objects include:

- Whiteboard
- Laptop
- Customer Survey
- Notebook
- Prototype
- Phone
- Calendar
- Financial Report

Clicking an object opens a dialogue window containing relevant information.

---

## 4.2 Investigation System

Each room contains several interactive objects.

Some objects provide critical clues, while others offer supporting information.

Players may investigate the room in any order before attempting the final challenge.

Not every object is required to complete the room, but exploring additional objects generally leads to better-informed decisions.

---

## 4.3 Candidate Notebook

As players discover important information, key findings are automatically recorded in the Candidate Notebook.

The notebook serves as a summary of all collected evidence and can be opened at any time during gameplay.

Example notebook entries:

- Customers complain about long waiting times.
- Target market consists primarily of university students.
- Peak sales occur during lunchtime.
- Competitors offer faster service.

The notebook reduces unnecessary backtracking while encouraging players to investigate thoroughly.

---

## 4.4 Decision-Making System

Each room concludes with one or more entrepreneurship challenges.

Players must select the most appropriate business decision based on the evidence they have collected.

Possible challenge formats include:

- Multiple Choice
- Ranking Priorities
- Matching Evidence
- Resource Allocation

Different answers receive different scores depending on the quality of entrepreneurial reasoning.

The game emphasizes informed decision-making rather than memorization.

---

## 4.5 Dialogue System

EVA guides players throughout the assessment.

Dialogue is used to:

- Introduce each challenge.
- Explain objectives.
- Provide hints.
- Deliver educational feedback.
- Present the final evaluation.

Dialogue appears inside a dedicated dialogue box and advances when the player clicks the Continue button.

---

## 4.6 Hint System

Players may request hints if they become stuck.

Hints are divided into three levels.

Level 1
- General guidance without revealing the solution.

Level 2
- Indicates which object or clue deserves further investigation.

Level 3
- Provides stronger guidance toward the correct reasoning.

Using hints reduces the maximum obtainable score for the room.

---

## 4.7 Scoring System

Every room is evaluated using four assessment components.

| Component | Maximum Points |
|------------|---------------:|
| Puzzle Completion | 10 |
| Decision Quality | 10 |
| Investigation | 10 |
| **Total** | **30** |

After completing all three rooms, bonus points are awarded based on remaining time.

| Component | Maximum Points |
|------------|---------------:|
| Time Bonus | 10 |

Maximum overall score:

**100 points**

Scores are calculated automatically and displayed immediately after the assessment.

---

## 4.8 Timer System

The game uses one global countdown timer.

Duration:

**20 minutes**

The timer remains visible throughout the assessment.

If the timer reaches zero, the assessment ends automatically and the team receives its final evaluation based on completed progress.

---

## 4.9 Room Progression

Rooms are completed sequentially.

Players cannot access the next room until the current room has been completed.

Progression:

The Idea Lab

↓

The Customer Hub

↓

The Founder Boardroom

↓

Final Evaluation

---

## 4.10 Founder Profile

At the end of the assessment, players receive a Founder Profile based on their overall performance.

The Founder Profile summarizes the team's entrepreneurial strengths and encourages self-reflection.

Example profiles include:

- Strategic Builder
- Customer Champion
- Visionary Innovator
- Opportunity Hunter
- Growth Explorer

The Founder Profile does not affect the final score but serves as personalized educational feedback.

---

# 5. Rooms Overview
## Overview

The Founder Assessment consists of **3 challenge rooms**, each representing a different stage of the entrepreneurial journey. Every room evaluates a specific entrepreneurial competency through an interactive scenario that combines exploration, investigation, teamwork, and decision-making.

Although each room has a different theme, all rooms follow the same gameplay structure:

1. Explore the environment.
2. Investigate interactive objects.
3. Gather evidence into the Candidate Notebook.
4. Discuss findings as a team.
5. Complete the entrepreneurial challenge.
6. Receive feedback and room score.
7. Unlock the next room.

Each room is designed to be completed in approximately **5–6 minutes**, allowing the entire game to be completed within the 20-minute time limit.

---

Opportunity Recognition

Recognizing business opportunities from real customer problems.

Students learn that successful businesses begin by identifying genuine customer pain points rather than immediately proposing solutions.

- Select the strongest opportunity.

Not all information is equally useful.

Candidates must distinguish assumptions from validated customer evidence.

## Primary Gameplay

- Review interview transcripts.
- Examine customer surveys.

# 6. Scoring System

## Overview

Escape Startup Lab uses an automated scoring system designed to objectively evaluate entrepreneurial thinking throughout the Founder Assessment.

Scores are calculated automatically by the game, eliminating the need for manual assessment by the committee.

The maximum possible score is **100 points**.

At the end of the game, the final score is displayed together with a room-by-room breakdown and a Founder Profile.

---

## Scoring Structure

The Founder Assessment consists of three challenge rooms.

Each room contributes **30 points**, resulting in a maximum of **90 points**.

An additional **10-point Performance Bonus** is awarded based on the team's overall consistency throughout the assessment.

| Component | Maximum Score |
|------------|--------------:|
| The Idea Lab | 30 |
| The Customer Hub | 30 |
| The Founder Boardroom | 30 |
| Performance Bonus | 10 |
| **TOTAL** | **100** |

---

## Room Scoring

Each room is evaluated using three assessment components.

| Assessment Component | Maximum Points |
|----------------------|---------------:|
| Puzzle Completion | 10 |
| Decision Quality | 10 |
| Evidence Collection | 10 |
| **Total** | **30** |

---

## Puzzle Completion (10 Points)

Measures whether the team successfully completes the room objective.

| Result | Points |
|---------|-------:|
| Challenge completed successfully | 10 |
| Challenge incomplete | 0 |

Puzzle Completion evaluates progress only and does not assess the quality of entrepreneurial reasoning.

---

## Decision Quality (10 Points)

Measures how well the team's final decision reflects entrepreneurial thinking.

Each answer has a predefined score.

Example:

| Decision | Points |
|-----------|-------:|
| Best entrepreneurial decision | 10 |
| Good decision | 7 |
| Acceptable decision | 4 |
| Poor decision | 0 |

Decision scores are determined during game design and remain consistent for every team.

---

## Evidence Collection (10 Points)

Throughout each room, teams investigate interactive objects.

Important discoveries are automatically recorded in the Candidate Notebook.

Each important clue is worth points.

Example:

| Important Evidence Found | Points |
|--------------------------|-------:|
| Customer Interview | 2 |
| Survey Results | 2 |
| Market Observation | 2 |
| Sales Report | 2 |
| Competitor Analysis | 2 |

Maximum:

**10 points**

Teams are encouraged to investigate the environment thoroughly before making decisions.

---

## Performance Bonus (10 Points)

The final bonus rewards consistent performance across the entire Founder Assessment.

| Requirement | Bonus |
|-------------|-------:|
| Complete all three rooms | 5 |
| Use no hints | 5 |

Maximum:

**10 points**

This bonus encourages careful investigation and independent problem solving.

---

The Founder Profile is generated separately from the numerical score.

It provides educational feedback by highlighting the team's entrepreneurial strengths.

Possible profiles include:

- Opportunity Hunter
- Customer Champion
- Strategic Builder
- Visionary Innovator

The Founder Profile does not affect the team's final score.

## Design Principles

The scoring system is designed to:

- Objectively assess entrepreneurial thinking.
- Reward exploration and evidence gathering.
- Encourage discussion before decision-making.
- Minimize subjective judgment.
- Provide immediate feedback.
- Support efficient score recording during rally-style competitions.

---

## Critical Evidence System

Not every interactive object contributes to the team's score.

Each challenge room contains a mixture of:

- **Critical Evidence** – Information required to make well-informed entrepreneurial decisions.
- **Supporting Evidence** – Additional information that provides context but does not award points.
- **Decorative Objects** – Objects included to create a realistic environment without affecting gameplay or scoring.

This design encourages players to think critically about which information is relevant rather than clicking every object indiscriminately.

---

## Critical Evidence

Critical Evidence represents the most valuable information available within a room.

Examples include:

- Customer interview transcripts
- Survey results
- Sales reports
- Competitor analysis
- Observation notes

Each Critical Evidence object automatically awards points when discovered and is recorded in the Candidate Notebook.

Example:

| Critical Evidence | Points |
|-------------------|-------:|
| Customer Interview | 2 |
| Survey Results | 2 |
| Sales Report | 2 |
| Competitor Analysis | 2 |
| Observation Notes | 2 |

Maximum Evidence Collection Score:

**10 points**

---

## Supporting Evidence

Supporting Evidence provides useful background information but does not directly award points.

Examples include:

- Company profile
- Product sketches
- Internal memos
- Industry news articles
- Marketing posters

Although these objects do not contribute to the score, they may help teams better understand the scenario and improve the quality of their final decision.

---

## Decorative Objects

Decorative objects exist solely to enhance immersion and create a believable startup environment.

Examples include:

- Coffee cup
- Office plant
- Wall clock
- Bookshelf
- Office chair
- Whiteboard markers

Decorative objects do not provide clues or points.

---

## Design Rationale

The Critical Evidence System is designed to encourage evidence-based decision making.

Rather than rewarding indiscriminate exploration, the game rewards teams that identify and prioritize information that is genuinely relevant to solving entrepreneurial problems.

This mechanic reflects real entrepreneurial practice, where founders must distinguish meaningful market evidence from background information before making strategic decisions.

# 10. Sound Design

Sound design in *Escape Startup Lab* is intended to enhance immersion, reinforce player interactions, and provide meaningful feedback without distracting from the educational objectives of the game.

The overall sound style should be clean, modern, and professional, reflecting the atmosphere of a startup innovation lab.

---

## Sound Design Principles

The game's audio design follows these principles:

- Simple and unobtrusive.
- Reinforces important player actions.
- Supports concentration and team discussion.
- Provides immediate feedback.
- Maintains a professional and educational atmosphere.

# 12. Educational Alignment Matrix

## Overview

Escape Startup Lab is designed as an educational game that develops entrepreneurial competencies through interactive problem-solving rather than traditional memorization.

Each challenge room is intentionally aligned with specific learning outcomes, entrepreneurial competencies, gameplay mechanics, and assessment methods. This alignment ensures that every gameplay activity contributes directly to the educational objectives of the Founder Assessment.

---

## Educational Alignment Matrix

| Challenge Room | Entrepreneurship Competency | Learning Outcome | Core Gameplay Mechanic | Assessment Method |
|----------------|-----------------------------|------------------|------------------------|-------------------|
| **The Idea Lab** | Opportunity Recognition | Identify real customer problems and recognize viable business opportunities. | Investigation, Evidence Collection, Critical Thinking | Opportunity Identification Challenge |
| **The Customer Hub** | Customer Discovery | Distinguish assumptions from validated customer evidence before making business decisions. | Information Analysis, Evidence Validation, Team Discussion | Customer Validation Challenge |
| **The Founder Boardroom** | Strategic Decision Making | Evaluate business alternatives and allocate limited resources effectively. | Resource Allocation, Decision Making, Prioritization | Strategic Business Challenge |

---

## Learning Progression

The game follows the natural entrepreneurial process.

```text
Recognize a Problem
        ↓
Validate the Problem
        ↓
Develop a Business Strategy
```

Rather than presenting isolated entrepreneurship concepts, each room builds upon the previous one to simulate the decision-making journey of a real entrepreneur.

---

## Competencies Assessed

The Founder Assessment evaluates the following entrepreneurial competencies.

| Competency | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| Opportunity Recognition | Identifying unmet customer needs and potential business opportunities. |
| Customer Discovery | Gathering and interpreting customer evidence before making decisions. |
| Critical Thinking | Evaluating information and distinguishing relevant evidence from distractions. |
| Strategic Decision Making | Selecting business strategies based on available evidence and constraints. |
| Resource Management | Prioritizing actions when resources are limited. |
| Team Collaboration | Discussing alternatives and reaching consensus through collaborative problem-solving. |

---

## Educational Design Principles

The game is developed according to the following educational principles.

### Learning by Doing

Students learn entrepreneurship through active participation rather than passive instruction.

---

### Evidence-Based Decision Making

Players are encouraged to collect, analyze, and evaluate information before making business decisions.

---

### Immediate Feedback

Every important decision receives constructive feedback explaining the entrepreneurial reasoning behind the outcome.

The objective is not simply to identify correct answers, but to understand why certain decisions are more effective than others.

---

### Collaborative Learning

The game is designed for small teams, encouraging discussion, negotiation, and shared decision-making throughout the assessment.

---

### Authentic Entrepreneurial Thinking

Challenges simulate realistic entrepreneurial situations that require observation, analysis, and strategic judgment rather than memorization of theoretical concepts.

---

## Assessment Philosophy

Escape Startup Lab measures entrepreneurial thinking rather than factual recall.

The assessment rewards players who:

- Investigate before acting.
- Support decisions with evidence.
- Collaborate effectively with teammates.
- Prioritize customer needs.
- Think strategically under constraints.

This approach reflects real entrepreneurial practice, where success depends on informed decision-making rather than remembering textbook definitions.

---

## Expected Learning Outcomes

Upon completing the Founder Assessment, participants are expected to be able to:

1. Identify customer problems that present potential business opportunities.
2. Differentiate assumptions from validated customer evidence.
3. Apply evidence-based reasoning when making entrepreneurial decisions.
4. Evaluate trade-offs when resources are limited.
5. Collaborate effectively to solve business problems.
6. Understand entrepreneurship as a structured problem-solving process rather than a sequence of isolated business concepts.

---

## Educational Impact

Escape Startup Lab transforms entrepreneurship learning into an interactive experience where students actively practice entrepreneurial competencies in a safe, engaging, and collaborative environment.

By integrating storytelling, investigation, evidence collection, and strategic decision-making, the game provides a meaningful learning experience while maintaining the excitement and engagement of an escape room challenge.

The Founder Assessment is intended to reinforce entrepreneurial mindsets that students can transfer to future academic projects, business competitions, and real-world entrepreneurial activities.
